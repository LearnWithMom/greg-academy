First Formation Scramble

12:00 PM
670 cal60g protein51g carb25g fatCook time 12 min

Ingredients

  • Large whole eggs3
  • Liquid egg whites1 cup (8 oz)
  • Fresh spinach, packed2 cups
  • Yellow onion, diced1/2 cup
  • Red bell pepper, diced1/2 cup
  • Olive oil1 tsp
  • Ranger Rub1 tsp
  • Reduced-fat sharp cheddar1 oz
  • Salsa or hot sauce, to finish2 tbsp
  • Sweet potato or russet, whole150 g
  • Cinnamon (sweet) or hot sauce (russet)1/4 tsp

Drink

  • Water16 oz
  • Black coffee or unsweet teaas desired

How to cook it

  1. Stab the potato six times with a fork. Sweet potato: microwave 6–7 minutes, or roast at 400°F for 45. Russet: microwave 8–9 minutes, or roast at 400°F for 55. Split it open and mash the inside right in the skin. Cinnamon and black pepper on a sweet potato tastes like dessert — on a russet, go hot sauce, cracked pepper and a pinch of salt.
  2. Nonstick skillet, medium-high, olive oil in. Onion and bell pepper for 3 minutes — leave them alone long enough to get some char on the edges. Char is flavor.
  3. Spinach in, toss 60 seconds until it collapses. Push everything to one side of the pan.
  4. Whisk the eggs and whites with the Ranger Rub until it's one color. Pour into the empty side, let it sit 20 seconds, then drag the set edges into the middle with a spatula. Big soft folds, not scrambled-to-death.
  5. Fold the vegetables back through while the egg is still glossy and slightly wet. Kill the heat — carryover finishes it. Dry eggs are a crime.
  6. Cheese over the top, lid on for 30 seconds to melt. Salsa and hot sauce on the plate. Sweet potato on the side.
Make it hit harderCook the onion and pepper in the pan first and don't rush it. Ninety percent of "healthy food tastes bad" is people dumping everything in a lukewarm pan at once. Hot pan, patience, finish with acid — lime, hot sauce, vinegar.

Southwest Chicken Power Bowl

3:00 PM
785 cal83g protein64g carb19g fatCook time 15 min

Ingredients

  • Chicken breast, cooked8 oz
  •   (raw weight)10 oz
  • Cooked jasmine or brown rice1/2 cup
  • Spinach or chopped romaine3 cups
  • Cucumber, sliced1/2 cup
  • Shredded carrot1/2 cup
  • Cherry tomatoes, halved1/2 cup
  • Black beans, rinsed1/4 cup
  • Raisins1 tbsp
  • Pico de gallo2 tbsp
  • Light vinaigrette or yogurt ranch2 tbsp
  • Lime1/2
  • Southwest Blend (for the chicken)1 tbsp
  • Olive oil (for the chicken)1 tsp

How to cook it

  1. Butterfly any breast thicker than a half-inch so it's even end to end. Uneven chicken means burnt on one side and raw in the middle.
  2. Pat it bone dry with paper towel. Wet chicken steams; it never browns. This is the single biggest thing most people skip.
  3. Rub with the olive oil, then the Southwest blend, pressed in with your palm.
  4. Grill or cast iron on high. Lay it down and don't touch it for 4 minutes. Flip once, 4–5 more minutes, pull at 165°F.
  5. Rest 5 minutes before you cut it. Cut it early and the juice ends up on the board instead of in the meat.
  6. Slice on a bias against the grain. Build the bowl: rice on the bottom, greens, then the cold veg, chicken fanned across the top. Pico, dressing, hard squeeze of lime.

Chow 3  ·  H+7:30

Garlic Herb Skillet, Butter Basted

7:30 PM
760 cal81g protein57g carb23g fatCook time 20 min

Protein — pick one

  • Chicken breast8 oz cooked
  • Turkey breast cutlets8 oz cooked
  • Sirloin, trimmed7 oz cooked
  • Salmon fillet6 oz cooked
  • Large shrimp, peeled10 oz

Seasoning & pan

  • Olive oil1 tsp
  • Ranger Rub1 tbsp
  • Garlic cloves, smashed3
  • Fresh thyme or rosemary2 sprigs
  • Butter (for basting)1 tsp
  • Lemon, to finish1/2

Vegetables — pick two

  • Roasted broccoli1 1/2 cups
  • Blistered green beans1 1/2 cups
  • Roasted asparagus1 1/2 cups
  • Roasted cauliflower1 1/2 cups
  • Garlic sautéed spinach4 cups raw

Carb — pick one

  • Roasted sweet potato wedges150 g
  • Baked russet potato150 g
  • Smashed baby red potatoes150 g
  • Cooked rice3/4 cup

Sauce — pick one, 2 tbsp

  • Chimichurri60 cal
  • Yogurt ranch20 cal
  • Chipotle crema25 cal
  • Lite teriyaki25 cal
  • Mustard-vinegar slap10 cal

How to cook it

  1. Pull the protein out of the fridge 20 minutes early. Cold meat in a hot pan cooks unevenly — burnt outside, cold center.
  2. Pat it completely dry. Oil it, then press the Ranger Rub into both sides. Season the meat, not the pan.
  3. Cast iron or a heavy skillet on medium-high for 2 full minutes empty, until it just barely starts to smoke.
  4. Lay it in away from you. Do not move it for 4 minutes. That's where the crust comes from. Every time you poke it you're starting over.
  5. Flip once. Now add the smashed garlic, the herb sprigs, and the butter. Tilt the pan and spoon that foaming garlic butter over the top for the last 60–90 seconds.
  6. Temps: chicken and turkey 165°F · sirloin 130–135°F for medium rare · salmon 125–130°F · shrimp until it curls into a C. A curled O means you cooked it too long.
  7. Rest 5 minutes on the board, then slice against the grain. Squeeze the lemon over it right before it goes on the plate.

Vegetables, done right

  1. Broccoli / cauliflower / asparagus: 425°F oven, 1/2 tsp oil, 1/2 tsp Ranger Rub, spread out on the sheet — crowded veg steams. Broccoli 20 min, cauliflower 25, asparagus 12. Pull them when the tips are almost black.
  2. Green beans: dry pan, high heat, 4 minutes untouched until they blister and pop, then 1 tsp oil, 2 sliced garlic cloves, red pepper flake, 2 more minutes.
  3. Spinach: 1 tsp oil, 3 sliced garlic cloves for 20 seconds, all 4 cups in at once with tongs, 90 seconds, splash of vinegar. Cooks down to about a cup.
Sweet potato or regular potato?Gram for gram it's close to a tie — about 90 calories per 100 g for sweet potato, 93 for a russet with the skin on. The big gap people quote comes from comparing a small sweet potato to a large russet. Sweet potato wins on vitamin A by a mile. Russet wins on potassium and has slightly more protein, and plain potatoes are the most filling food ever put through a satiety study. Weigh it and eat the one you'll still want on day 22.
The one move that changes everythingButter basting at the end. One teaspoon of butter, garlic, and a herb sprig spooned over the meat for the last minute costs you 35 calories and turns a boring chicken breast into something you'd pay for. Do it every night.

Still Hungry?

Inside the window only

Eat as much of this as you want. None of it will move the needle:

  • Any non-starchy vegetable — broccoli, cauliflower, green beans, peppers, cucumber, celery, spinach, cabbage, zucchini
  • Broth, pickles, sauerkraut, mustard, hot sauce, vinegar, herbs, lemon and lime
  • Black coffee, unsweet tea, sparkling water, sugar-free drinks

Then, if you're still hungry, add one of these:

  • Protein shake in water30 g protein
  • Hard-boiled eggs2
  • Plain chicken breast4 oz
  • Nonfat Greek yogurt1 cup

Outside the Window

The fast is the easy part — protect it

  • Yes: water, black coffee, plain unsweet tea, sparkling water, electrolytes with no sugar, plain sugar-free gum
  • No: cream, sugar, juice, soda, alcohol, "just a bite" of anything

If you're hungry in hour 14, drink 20 oz of cold water and wait ten minutes. Nine times out of ten it passes. Hunger comes in waves, it doesn't build forever — that's the part nobody tells you.

Bad day protocolIf a day goes sideways — field problem, family thing, funeral, whatever — you don't restart the 30 days. You eat the next meal on plan. One bad meal is a bad meal. Three bad days in a row is a decision. Don't let it become a decision.

House Rules for Flavor

Read this once, use it forever

  • Hot pan, dry meat. Everything else is details.
  • Finish with acid. Lemon, lime, or vinegar at the end makes food taste seasoned when it's actually just bright.
  • Char one thing. Blistered veg beats boiled veg every single time.
  • Rest the meat 5 minutes. Free juiciness.
  • Salt is not the enemy here. You're not on a sodium restriction — you're on a calorie deficit. Season the food.

Free Flavor

Unlimited · use as heavy as you want

Dry: garlic powder, onion powder, smoked paprika, cumin, chili powder, Italian seasoning, lemon pepper, Cajun and blackened blends, any Mrs. Dash, oregano, thyme, rosemary, dill, red pepper flake, black pepper, cinnamon.

Wet: Frank's RedHot, Cholula, Tabasco, yellow and dijon mustard, all vinegars, lemon and lime juice, salsa and pico, low-sodium soy sauce, Worcestershire, horseradish, fresh garlic, ginger, jalapeño, cilantro, green onion.

Measure these: BBQ sauce (even sugar-free), teriyaki, ketchup, sriracha, honey mustard, and anything creamy. Two tablespoons, not a pour.

Sustainment

Prep Day

Two hours on Sunday, two hours on Wednesday. That's the whole secret. A man who has 8 oz of cooked chicken sitting in a container does not order Whataburger at 2100.

The Two-Hour Block

Sunday · covers days 1–4

  1. Oven to 425°F. Get two sheet trays out.
  2. Tray 1: 10 sweet potatoes, poked with a fork. Tray 2: 3 lb chicken breast, butterflied, oiled, coated in Ranger Rub. Chicken comes out at 22–25 min (165°F). Potatoes need 45–50.
  3. While the oven runs: 3 cups dry rice into the rice cooker. That's about 9 cups cooked — four days of 3/4-cup portions with room to spare.
  4. Boil 12 eggs. Cold water to start, bring to a boil, kill the heat, lid on for 11 minutes, straight into an ice bath. They peel clean every time.
  5. Mix all four spice blends into small jars. Label them. Unlabeled brown powder is how dinner gets ruined.
  6. Make the chimichurri and the yogurt ranch. Both keep 5 days.
  7. Portion 8 oz of chicken with 1/2 cup rice into four containers. Wash and dry the greens, bag them with a paper towel inside so they don't turn to slime.
  8. Wednesday night, run it again for days 5–7. Nothing in the fridge past four days.

Gear That Earns Its Space

Not optional, honestly

  • A digital food scale. $12. Every "8 oz" on this page is a guess without one, and guessing is how 30 days of work turns into 20.
  • An instant-read thermometer. $15. It's the difference between juicy chicken and rubber.
  • Cast iron skillet. The crust you get off cast iron is why this food tastes like food.
  • 10–12 glass containers. Glass doesn't stain, doesn't hold smell, goes straight in the microwave.
  • A gallon jug. Fill it in the morning, empty it by the time the window closes. No math.
Prep-day mindsetYou're not cooking dinner on Sunday. You're removing every decision from the next four days, so that on the day you're smoked, tired, and irritated, the easy choice and the right choice are the same choice.

Contingency

When You Can't
Cook

Staff duty, the field, a 12-hour day, the wife's birthday dinner. The plan has to survive contact. Here's how you stay on it without a kitchen.

DFAC

Hit the grill line, ask for a double portion of the grilled chicken or the roast beef. Skip the gravy and everything from the fry basket. One scoop of rice or a baked potato, no butter. Then load the salad bar with everything green and hit it with vinegar and a little oil. Water, not the juice machine.

Chick-fil-A

12-count grilled nuggets, kale crunch side, and a second order of grilled nuggets if it's your Chow 2. Water or unsweet tea. That's about 500 calories and 60 g of protein.

Chipotle

Bowl: double chicken, white rice, black beans, fajita veggies, both salsas, lettuce. No queso, no sour cream, no chips. Nearly identical numbers to Chow 2.

Subway

Foot-long oven-roasted turkey or rotisserie chicken on wheat, double meat, no cheese, no mayo, every vegetable they have, red wine vinegar and a little oil, black pepper.

Wendy's

Grilled chicken sandwich, no mayo, plus a large chili. Around 600 calories, 55 g of protein, and it's on every road you'll drive.

Field / MRE

Eat the entrée and the crackers. Leave the dessert, the cheese spread, and the sugar drink powders — keep the electrolyte ones. Bring your own protein bars and jerky in the assault pack. Two MREs a day plus your own protein is roughly on plan.

Restaurant

Sirloin or grilled chicken, ask them to double the vegetables instead of the potato, sauce and dressing on the side. Water while everybody else orders. You can eat anywhere on this plan — you just can't eat everything.

Gas station

Beef jerky, a bag of pistachios, a protein shake, string cheese, a banana. Not a great meal. Better than every other option in the building.

The standard

As of 7 July 2026 the Army scrapped the height/weight tables, the old tape test, and supplemental body fat assessments. The only standard now is waist-to-height ratio, and it has to come in under 0.55. Waist at the navel, divided by height in inches.

Your number

Height in inches × 0.55 = your maximum waist. At 6'0" (72") that's 39.5". At 6'1" it's 40.1". At 5'11" it's 39.0". Measure your actual height before you trust any target — one inch of height is worth half an inch of waist.

Two other changes

DEXA, BodPod, and InBody scans are no longer authorized to challenge a waist-to-height result, so there's no scan to fall back on. And no separation actions will be taken for failing it during the Army's initial 180-day assessment period. If you measure 0.55 or over, a second team re-measures you the same duty day.

How to measure

Same day each week, first thing in the morning, after the latrine, before you eat or drink anything. Tape at the navel, parallel to the deck, snug without compressing. Relaxed stomach at the end of a normal breath. Don't suck in and don't push out — you're not measuring your ability to hold your breath, you're measuring progress. Divide by your height in inches and write down the ratio, not just the waist.

Realistic math

Expect 8–15 lb and roughly 1–2 inches off the waist in 30 days at this size. Week one moves fast because a lot of it is water and glycogen. Weeks two and three slow down and that is not failure — that's when actual fat is leaving.

Water

One gallon a day, every day, including test day. Dehydrating yourself before a tape doesn't shrink your waist, it just makes you weak and dizzy for the PT test. Keep the water and the sodium steady.

Steps

10,000 a day on top of PT. This is the cheapest inch on the list — it burns fat without adding fatigue or appetite the way extra hard cardio does. Take calls walking. Park at the far end of the lot.

Lifting

Keep lifting 3 days a week if you already do. When you're in a deficit, lifting is the signal that tells your body to hold onto muscle and give up fat instead. Don't trade the weight room for more cardio.

Sleep

Seven hours. Short sleep raises cortisol and appetite, and on a man your size it shows up right around the middle. Sleep is not the soft part of this plan.

Alcohol

Zero for 30 days. It's empty calories and it stalls fat burning for about 48 hours per session. Thirty days is nothing. Take the thirty days.

Don't eat less

Cutting below this on purpose will cost you muscle, and muscle is a big part of what's holding your waist in. It also makes you likelier to quit in week two. Consistency for 30 straight days beats a harder plan you abandon on day 9. Every single time.

One flag

If you're on blood pressure, blood sugar, or heart medication, run the fasting window past your provider first — those doses sometimes need adjusting when meal timing changes. Lightheaded, dizzy, or heart racing means eat something and get it looked at. Passing the tape isn't worth a profile.

Day 30

Read this on day 14 when you want to quit

Greg — you've done harder things than this on less sleep and with worse odds. This isn't a diet, it's a 30-day mission with a defined objective, a plan of execution, and a measurable end state. You already know how to run one of those.

The men who fail this don't fail because the food was wrong. They fail because on day 11 they decided one bad night meant the whole thing was blown. It doesn't. You eat the next meal on plan and you keep marching.

Thirty days. One standard. Go get it.
